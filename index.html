Research

SHANK3, before the synapse

Scroll to follow SHANK3 from the synapse, where it is usually studied, to the nucleolus, where my work begins.

The established view

At the synapse

SHANK3 is one of the best-established autism genes, and its haploinsufficiency is considered the main cause of the neurological features of Phelan-McDermid syndrome.

Most of what is known about it comes from the synapse, where it scaffolds the postsynaptic density and anchors receptor complexes.

In Fig. 1, the berry points along the dendrites represent synapses.

My focus

In the nucleolus, before any synapse

My work looks earlier in development, in human induced pluripotent stem cells, neural progenitors and brain organoids. In these cells, SHANK3 is enriched in the nucleolus, where it regulates SOX2 expression and the pace of proliferation and differentiation.

SHANK3 insufficiency may therefore disturb development before a single synapse has formed.

Illustration. SHANK3 (red) concentrates in the nucleolus, ringed by nucleolin (green), inside a nucleus stained with Hoechst (blue).

Laboratory software

Expression Factory

RT-qPCR, from plate to figure. Ct values you can check, and nothing hidden.

A desktop application for RT-qPCR, used in my laboratory. It covers the whole experiment: plate layout, pipetting and master-mix sheets, import of the instrument run, analysis and figures.

Its engine calls Ct values that match the instrument software to the last digit, and every excluded well or correction stays visible. It also designs primers, maintains the lab's shared primer library and tracks cell cultures from thaw to cDNA.

Side project

reLive

Windows Live Messenger, running again.

In my spare time I am rebuilding Windows Live Messenger. My server implements the original MSNP protocol, and the web and Android clients preserve the glossy 2009 interface, with nudges, scenes, group chats, and voice and video calls.

Most of the work lies in reverse-engineering an undocumented protocol. Signing in and hearing that sound again never gets old.
